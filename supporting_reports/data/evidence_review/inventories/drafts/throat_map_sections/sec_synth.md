## 2. General response rules (summary)

Each rule states how the physics responds to an engineering choice in the
spherical warped-product class, with its evidence grade. Details and
citations are in the knob sections and section 7.

### 2.1 Identity-backed for the class

1. **Only areal-radius shaping moves radial null energy directly.**
   8πT(k,k) = −(2/R)k^ak^b∇_a∇_bR (I2). Lapse, stretch and shift enter the
   radial NEC only through the null rays along which R is differentiated.
   Measured corroboration: of four receiver actuation channels, "The only
   tested knob that moves selected-null is angular capacity" (G24); the local
   areal partner trades radial null against angular/current in the sign I2
   predicts (G14).
2. **Type IV demand requires time dependence acting where R varies.**
   Δ_rad = T(k₊,k₊)T(k₋,k₋) (I3); a static geometry has equal null energies.
   Measured: support decompression acting on the varying Ellis radius carries
   ~98% of the integrated Type IV burden (G5); matched static controls are all
   Type I; holding R constant where the metric evolves removes every Type IV
   point (G31). Corollaries, each identity-backed: static surrogates cannot
   see Type IV (I22); uniform slowdown thins but cannot remove the layer at an
   enthalpy zero with current (I9); boosting infrastructure preserves the
   discriminant (I26); a Type I regulator changes the source (I24).
3. **Constant R turns the radial block into an exact string cloud** with
   ρ = −p_l = 1/(8πR²), j = 0, boost-invariant, and moves every (α, β, A)
   dynamic into p_Ω = −K/8π, whose NEC fails where K > 1/R² (I4, G31).
4. **Throat tension depends on the throat radius alone.** p_r = −1/(8πR₀²)
   at R′ = 0 (I17). Measured invariance of the live p_l peak under V (2–15),
   η_N, catch timing, shock absorber, shell amplitude and w_th; exact
   (1.75/2)² scaling with R_th (G1, G2, G6).
5. **Tension and opening are separate duties.** Tension (W-term) is
   null-neutral; opening needs ρ+p_r = −R″/(4πR) < 0 (I16, I17). Flare-out to
   R′/A = 1 costs an integrated null deficit ≥ 1 per end; transition width sets
   only the peak, ∝ 1/width (I18, G31).
6. **Occupant safety is an algebraic cone condition; its repair costs appear
   through derivatives.** The carried worldline is timelike iff
   v₋ < v < v₊ (I5, I6); raising α or re-matching β buys margin pointwise
   (I7) while the demand responds to their gradients (measured in G3, G9,
   G26). Proper-time rate dτ/dσ = √(−norm); no report translates it into
   passenger aging.
7. **Shift demand is radial momentum.** j_l is affine in β with a 1/α factor
   and Δρ quadratic with 1/α² (PDF eqs. (9)–(14), INVENTORY DERIVATION); where
   B′ = Ḃ = 0 a shift produces no j_l. Measured: "The live service signal is
   ∆jl , not ∆ρ" (PDF p.5); small shift actuators route demand
   sign-symmetrically (G18).
8. **Clock shaping moves angular and strong-energy stress.** X = A″/A and
   Y = A′R′/(AR) feed p_t; an interior lapse maximum needs ρ+p_r+2p_t < 0 (I16,
   I19). Measured direction: a lapse-only receiver edit raised support-edge
   angular burden 33% (G24); coordinated lapse release moved lapse-derivative
   burden into the live tube (G15).
9. **Radial bundle focusing is set by shift shear and α/√A shear, never by B**
   (I11); measured mitigation ranking agrees (G26).
10. **Cone tipping (g_σσ ≥ 0) is ergo-like, not trapping** (I5); 39–42% of live
    samples tipped while every core seed escaped (G27) [L4].
11. **Join regularity sets stress growth**: a d^p cusp gives d^{p−2} stress;
    a slope jump a surface term; C2 finite (I13, G28).
12. **Disjoint edits superpose exactly** (locality of G_μν): bilateral receiver
    = sum of one-sided effects to six digits (G25).

### 2.2 Measured on one design (DESIGN), with the strength of the pattern

- **Radius broadening lowers pointwise peaks (∝ R⁻²) and raises integrated
  demand**, and lowers the 1+1 quantum supply-to-demand ratio (−8.45% at 2.05).
  Seen in four independent comparisons across two eras (G1). Strong pattern.
- **Endpoint load follows the time choreography of shift removal, not the
  spatial thickness of endpoint windows**: fade width 0.25 → 0.75 cut the reset
  cap 24% with ~80% transfer to the support edge; a 53% edge-width increase
  moved J by 1.5×10⁻⁵ relative (G22, G23). One grid.
- **Transition width matters more than smoothness order**: narrow
  finite-support smoothstep profiles tripled live null burden against tanh;
  wide ones cut ∂²γ_ll by 70% (G16). Consistent with G being linear in second
  derivatives; not derived.
- **Packet-local carving trades live exposure against total burden and causal
  margin** when α, A, β share one support scalar (G8–G10); the causal cost is
  a design-family identity (§3.B header), the magnitudes are measured.
- **Channel separation on the tuned design**: radial null ← catch timing;
  radial pressure ← w_th and R_th; packet safety ← lapse cushion; V headroom
  ← a sharp cliff (G7). The p_l separation is explained by rule 4; the rest
  are measured.
- **Live p_l fraction is fixed by the carve footprint to 5–6 digits across V
  and lapse settings** (G8); **the non-live core null peak is V-independent**
  (G6). Measured regularities without derivation.
- **Compactly supported actuators keep occupant readouts quiet; tails leak**:
  raised-cosine vs smooth box at matched strength, packet drift 3.09e−8 vs
  1.81e−2 (G21).
- **Wider collars improve optical focusing and live null exposure while
  placing negative energy density in the packet frame** (G26).
- **Source-architecture patterns** (static surrogate): mechanical
  independence costs support energy (+77%) and charge inventory (×2) for
  +2.48% total energy (S1); independent channel counts cut inventory ~17× and
  reflector forces 30–60× (S4); reflection localizes a useful sign at a
  confinement load orders of magnitude larger (S8); every fix to a population
  boundary relocates residual demand to the new boundary (S5–S7).
