# Proposed structure (derived from 02_SYNTHESIS)

The structure follows the discipline the evidence defines: inverse design of
spacetime geometry. The ordering follows the dependency the incident record
shows. Global structure and class-level exclusions come before construction,
identities come before numerics, and occupant and causal requirements travel
with every design. Project results appear only as general identities, or as
labelled examples next to the literature's standard geometries.

## Part I. Foundations

1. **Geometry as the engineered object.** Lorentzian geometry, causal
   structure, proper time. A performance claim is made inside one spacetime:
   arrival against the earliest signal through the same background (A4).
2. **The 3+1 split.** Lapse, shift, spatial metric, extrinsic curvature,
   constraints. The worked example is a static wormhole (Morris–Thorne) next
   to a flat-slice warp metric (Alcubierre, Natário).
3. **Stress-energy and its algebra.** Hawking–Ellis types with their current
   scope (Type III excluded by symmetry; Type IV a test-field effect;
   back-reaction forcing Type I). Pointwise energy conditions and the
   theorem that QFT violates them.
4. **Quantum energy bounds, with exact scopes.** Worldline QEIs, null QEIs
   (bounded along timelike worldlines, none along null geodesics in 4D),
   quantum interest, the SNEC and DSNEC, the QNEC, ANEC in flat and curved
   space, and self-consistent achronal ANEC as a conjecture.
5. **Global structure.** Topology change, topological censorship (assumes
   ANEC), chronology and its protection conjecture, time functions against
   global hyperbolicity, and faster-than-light theorems with their
   definitions (Olum; Gao–Wald with the Galloway null line; Krasnikov).
6. **Semiclassical response.** The trace anomaly, horizon temperatures and
   the Tolman relation, vacuum polarization, the species bound, and the
   validity limits of semiclassical gravity.
7. **The landscape of engineered geometries.** Alcubierre, Natário, Van Den
   Broeck, Krasnikov and Everett–Roman, Morris–Thorne, thin shells,
   Bobrick–Martire, the 2021–26 positive-energy debate and its resolution.

## Part II. The inverse problem

8. **From geometry to demand.** T = G/8π read as a specification. Frames,
   observers, and why the geometry fixes the demand (A7).
9. **Testing the demand.** Complete tensor, all observers, time-dependent
   geometry, the transition to vacuum (A1). Zero momentum ⇒ Type I, so
   static and irrotational checks are blind to Type IV. Energy conditions as
   matrix inequalities. Type as a match to source families (A2).
10. **Global structure first.** Topology and ends, the exterior, the
    causality class, and the reference path for performance (A3, A4).
11. **Occupants.** The observables: clock rate against exterior time and
    light's crossing time, tides, acceleration, radiation temperature,
    swept matter (A10). The flat-compartment identity.

## Part III. How the physics responds to design (the core)

One chapter per class of metrics. Each gives its identities, its design
matrix (knob × channel, each entry marked identity or measured) and its
strategy–price table (02_SYNTHESIS §2.4).

12. **Flat slices with shift and lapse.** Shear carries all the energy; the
    lapse carries stress without energy; α⁻² suppression; the complete
    tensor; the shear and flux identities; the lapse-only NEC lemma; speed as
    a lapse contrast; time staging; vorticity and momentum.
13. **Static slicing and the lapse.** Morris–Thorne redshift function,
    lapse-supported NEC violation (Visser–Kar–Dadhich), hollow cores with a
    released lapse (Bolívar et al.), clock identities.
14. **Spherical warped products and throats.** The areal-radius Hessian,
    the radial discriminant, string clouds, flare-out cost, throat tension,
    thin shells with the equation of state as stability knob, and the
    long-versus-short dichotomy.
15. **Curved slices.** Pockets and the ³R energy channel, positive-mass
    shells with interior shift, and their boundary costs.
16. **Moving structures.** Pattern-frame Killing energy, light surfaces and
    horizons, the Mach-cone and flank conditions, lapse cavities, swept
    matter and its release, semiclassical instability of white fronts
    (A9).
17. **Scaling laws.** Size, speed, wall thickness under QEIs,
    shape-flattening results.
18. **Decomposition.** Design matrices, the conditions under which knobs
    decouple, adjustment order, and decoupling as a heuristic (C3).

## Part IV. Supplying the demand

19. **Exclusions first.** Class-level and scale-free exclusions, each with
    its exact scope: QEI field counts with the species bound, achronal ANEC
    for global leads, mirror and holder costs, curvature-coupling
    bound-state tests. Normalize to an absolute scale (A5).
20. **Net supply.** NEC-satisfying hardware only adds to the null deficit.
    Accounting boundaries and gain chains (A6).
21. **Ordinary matter under relativistic stress.** Strength-to-energy
    ratios, Maxwell and string tensors, storage limits, junction surface
    stress.
22. **Classical NEC-violating fields.** Kinetic braiding, Galileons,
    Horndeski and beyond, their stability, superluminality and
    UV-completion status, and their algebraic range (Type IV only where the
    NEC fails).
23. **Assemblies.** Component tensors, one shared geometry, interaction
    contracts, recoil, and steering by radiation (Bondi balance).
24. **Realizability as a design objective.** The transformation-optics
    lesson: among equivalent geometries choose the easiest to source, and
    forward-check the simplified source on every observable (C6).

## Part V. Dynamics

25. Back-reaction, stability and evolution: full numerical-relativity
    evolution of a warp drive (Clough et al.), semiclassical instability,
    and open problems.

## Part VI. Engineering method

26. **Verification.** Manufactured solutions and observed order, the grid
    convergence index, interval certification between samples, degenerate
    tensors, domain coverage, surrogates that forbid failure modes (B1–B7).
27. **Design studies.** Attribution against sensitivity maps, matched
    strength, set-based forks, single-gate distortion, absolute against
    relative measures (C1–C5).
28. **Credibility and claim scope.** Naming results by the check passed,
    multi-axis credibility, the kinematic/dynamic split, readiness. A sidebar
    on program hazards: frame review, stopping rules at two levels, live
    negative-verdict ledgers (D1–D5).

## Appendices

- A. Laboratory analogues: analogue gravity, laboratory negative-energy
  modalities, Casimir and QET experiments.
- B. Computational companion: the general toolkit modules, wrapped.
- C. Exercises: the 168 foundations and literature items of the quiz bank,
  plus new items on Parts II–IV.

## Placement decisions to confirm

- The method chapters (Part VI) could come right after Part II so that
  readers learn verification before the design classes. The evidence
  supports either order. The incident record argues that verification
  habits are needed from the first numerical chapter.
- Measured-only (DES) results appear as boxed examples with their
  single-design label, never in chapter text as laws.
- Reader and format are still open. The material now spans graduate GR,
  QFT in curved space and engineering method; a two-level layout (core text
  plus derivation boxes) would serve both GR students and physics-literate
  engineers.
