# Source feasibility and finite-assembly research direction

17 September 2026. Direction agreed after the source-first review and the
C1 angular-scalar spectral investigation.

The active investigation prioritizes a physically normalized source and
finite assembly accounting. Geometry, source family and finite architecture
inform one another. Detailed component optimization follows credible combined
source feasibility and the relevant dynamical checks.

The transport reference defines useful service behavior and the demanded
stress-energy. C1 remains the provisional arrangement of mechanically
independent multicomponent assemblies with overlapping active regions.
The [topology decision](RAIL_BUILD_TOPOLOGY_DECISION.md) and
[component assignments](RAIL_COMPONENT_CROSS_REFERENCE_AND_JOINT_COORDINATION.md)
remain the architectural basis.

## Investigation order

| Stage | Decision required | Appropriate evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Transport target | Identify useful geometry and service constraints. | Packet motion, timing, causal structure and demanded tensor. |
| Physical source contribution | Determine whether specified fields and materials can contribute the required signs, scale and spatial distribution. | Normalized tensors or decisive bounds, fixed physical parameters, state and confinement assumptions. |
| Finite C1 construction | Combine components across a finite assembly and one neighboring overlap. | Shared geometry; material and field tensors counted once; traction, current, momentum, angular momentum and exterior exchanges accounted. |
| Coupled dynamics | Establish admissible preparation, handoff and recovery. | Field/material response, module recoil, stability, causal timing and finite storage/heat budgets. |
| Detailed realization | Refine mechanisms whose feasibility requirements are sufficiently established. | Materials, fixtures, coatings, routing, losses and controls at the justified level of detail. |

Microscopic information enters as early as its consequences require. Mode
count, confinement, material stress and achievable reflection can determine
the sign or scale of a source. Such inputs belong in the feasibility stage.
Detailed implementation choices become useful as the combined construction
stabilizes. Existing component results remain reusable evidence within their
recorded assumptions.

## Common accounting and geometry feedback

Each physical sector retains a specified duty and its complete tensor. A
component's additional stress channels enter the combined equation even when
its principal duty concerns one direction. Ordinary support, radial quantum
stress, angular quantum stress, transition material, currents and reservoirs
keep their distinct responsibilities.

The first combined C1 test includes an overlapping module pair and declared
exterior conditions. Material boundaries, field extents and service boundaries
are specified separately. Permitted field and packet exchanges contribute to
the module motion and the external budgets. The combined source is evaluated
on one shared geometry.

Geometry remains a design variable. A fixed reference supports interpretable
comparisons; a measured source or boundary burden can motivate a bounded
change to the lapse, radius, transition or placement. Such a change carries
an explicit record of geometry, source placement, overlap and normalization,
together with the applicable service checks. Earlier exclusions retain the
source laws and geometry families under which they were established.

## Current decision

**Does the angular field make a useful, physically normalized contribution
when combined with the retained radial source and accounted boundary/material
demands?**

The [angular scalar spectral result](C1_ANGULAR_SCALAR_INVESTIGATION.md) provides
a specified field action and positive finite-domain mode evidence. The current
decision concerns its actual quantum stress and counted interactions. A
source comparison therefore records:

1. The field normalization, multiplicity, state, renormalization prescription
   and material response, with fixed parameters shared across the calculation.
2. The supplied tensor or controlled bounds in the regions carrying the
   assigned angular and transition demands.
3. Its combination with the retained radial and Maxwell sources, including
   the material and boundary budget and separately identified exterior duties.
4. The uncertainty that determines whether the candidate advances, requires
   a specific revision or reaches its stopping condition.

An algebraic allocation identifies a target. A positive mode spectrum
supports state construction. A supplied source contribution addresses the
required stress and its physical costs. Complete finite closure and coupled
dynamical feasibility have their own evidence requirements.

## Continuation and stopping criteria

Advance a candidate when its normalized contribution survives the relevant
numerical controls and leaves a specified, credible finite material/exchange
budget. Escalate the calculation when a remaining uncertainty could change
that decision. A favorable limiting approximation establishes the region and
conditions for its use; the full construction retains its boundary and state
dependence.

A wrong contribution sign, insufficient normalized strength or incompatible
combined material burden constrains the evaluated candidate and assumptions.
The next response targets the identified field, interface or geometric cause
while preserving the other component duties. Repeated fitting or refinement
ends when it leaves that cause unchanged. Detailed rotor, fixture, routing
and loss optimization resumes when its input requirements are supported by
the combined source and operating construction.
