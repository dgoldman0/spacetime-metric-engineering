## Magnetic channels: a controlled source mechanism

[Maldacena, Milekhin and Popov](https://arxiv.org/html/1807.04726v3)
construct a semiclassical throat supported by magnetic flux and charged
massless fermions. The lowest Landau level gives \(q\) longitudinal
channels per Dirac species. Field lines close through the throat and the
ambient exterior, creating the negative Casimir contribution. Equations
(2.3), (5.19), and (7.58) give

\[
R_0^2\simeq\frac{\pi q^2\eta}{e^2},\qquad
\ell=\frac{16R_0^3}{\eta c},\qquad
c=qN_f,\qquad A_{\rm center}\simeq\frac{R_0}{\ell}.
\]

Our same-radius comparison, using \(\eta=2.41279\times10^{-5}\),
\(e=0.1\), and one species, gives continuous flux \(q=23.4264\),
\(\ell=240167.7\), and \(A_{\rm center}=8.4923\times10^{-6}\).
Flux quantization would require an adjacent integer with a corresponding
parameter adjustment. The calculation serves as a scale comparison.
The current central lapse exceeds this value by \(8.1654\times10^6\)
when both clocks use the exterior normalization. Furthermore, the Maxwell
row supplies much more angular pressure than the demanded core ratio
\(p_t/\rho=0.003413\).

The published geometry therefore supplies an analytic benchmark for a
different balance between source strength and clock slowing. Magnetic
flux already fixes part of the gravitational load; its degeneracy and
the radius enter the same equation.

[Maldacena and Milekhin's larger construction](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.06618v2)
uses a Randall–Sundrum sector with a large effective two-dimensional
central charge. It offers a specified amplification mechanism beyond
the Standard Model. The construction still uses a redshifted throat,
an exterior return path, and an energy limit on transmitted matter.
It supplies a comparison for theories with additional degrees of freedom.

## Vortex strings: the closer material pattern

[Fu, Grado-White and Marolf](https://arxiv.org/html/1908.03273v2)
use quantum fluctuations of a string wrapping a noncontractible cycle
to make a charged wormhole traversable. Another string holds the mouths
apart. Their nonextremal solutions have a limited traversal window and
exponentially fragile support; they calculate the relevant perturbative
backreaction and transit times. Their approximation requires small total
gravitational string loading and sufficiently large individual tension
to control transverse fluctuations.

The loop construction also appears explicitly in
[Emparan, Grado-White, Marolf and Tomašević](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.07821v2),
section 3.1, where string zero modes can replace flux-guided fermions in
the Casimir sector. These papers establish a longitudinal source route
with mechanical tension included in the setup. Finite exterior support
for the rail would require its own mechanical solution.

A microscopic material basis is supplied by
[Witten's superconducting strings](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(85)90022-7).
Broken gauge theories can contain vortex cores with bound fermionic
zero modes. [Weinberg's index calculation](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.24.2669)
relates the zero-mode count to Higgs winding. This suggests an extension
of the screened-condensate direction in which the Higgs structure confines
longitudinal light modes. It is an inference combining these ingredients;
the existing spherical condensate has neither vortex winding nor the
required fermionic sector.

The useful quantity to identify in a microscopic example is the light
mode count per unit string tension, together with its width and spectrum
around a complete loop. Populating a superconducting current adds an
occupied-state contribution. The negative Casimir component comes from
the specified vacuum. A bulk mass gap, vortex winding, loop spin structure,
and interactions jointly determine whether a light channel survives.

## An analytic filter from the existing lapse

The Weyl transformation of the two-dimensional stress tensor, given in
MMP's appendix F, supplies a cheap comparison before a new material solve.
Let a static ideal conformal channel with balanced left- and right-moving
sectors have central charge \(c\), proper coordinate \(l\), lapse \(A\),
and total optical circumference

\[
L_{\rm opt}=\oint\frac{dl}{A},\qquad a=\log A.
\]

For the cylinder ground state, the two-dimensional orthonormal components
are

\[
\rho_2=-\frac{\pi c}{6L_{\rm opt}^2A^2}
       +\frac{c}{24\pi}(2a''+a'^2),\qquad
p_2=-\frac{\pi c}{6L_{\rm opt}^2A^2}
       -\frac{c}{24\pi}a'^2.
\]

Consequently an angularly averaged collection gives

\[
\rho_Q+p_{r,Q}=
\frac{c}{4\pi R^2}
\left[\frac{a''}{12\pi}
-\frac{\pi}{3L_{\rm opt}^2A^2}\right].
\]

This formula includes the curvature contribution as well as the cylinder
Casimir term. It is a leading conformal-channel model. Four-dimensional
vacuum polarization, massive modes, finite vortex cores, and additional
couplings carry further source terms. Chiral currents and time-dependent
states also require their energy flux.

Our evaluation at the rail throat gives
\(a''_0=7.69483\times10^{-4}\). Negative radial null stress in this
model requires

\[
L_{\rm opt}<\frac{2\pi}{A_0\sqrt{a''_0}}=3.26645.
\]

The condition is invariant under a constant rescaling of the time
coordinate. For a channel traversing any specified coordinate interval,
the integral over that interval is a lower bound on its complete optical
circumference. Setting the exterior return length to zero gives the most
negative cylinder term for that interval.

vanishes in the long-throat approximation. This is a direct literature
analogue of the stress-selection problem, and gives a useful limiting
benchmark.

[Kain's dynamical study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11217)

provide comparison data, with quantum normalization and dynamical
stability preceding any rail fit.
