# Joint source populations and distinct C1 source meshes

17 September 2026.

The retained radial conformal channels and four-dimensional angular scalar
admit more sampled source allocations when their populations vary
independently. The calculation includes both transition witnesses and
several angular cavity arrangements. Its ordinary-material remainder is an
admissibility allowance; constitutive material, complete scalar-wall stress,
exterior states and module motion remain construction requirements.

Two quantum source laws supply all the tabulated trials. Their relative
contributions vary between throat, overlap and transitions. Population
grouping reduces the number of adjustable settings while increasing some
inventory and interface demands. Source laws, population controls and
physical burdens therefore provide separate measures of complexity.

## Source laws and joint constraint

At each sampled point the source equation is

\[
\frac{G_{\hat a\hat b}}{8\pi}-T^{\rm EM}_{\hat a\hat b}
=T^{\rm radial}_{\hat a\hat b}
+\sum_s N_s T^{\phi,s}_{\hat a\hat b}+T^{\rm material}_{\hat a\hat b}.
\]

Each source contributes its complete \((\rho,p_r,p_t)\) tensor. The Maxwell
field, radial strip vacuum and conformally coupled scalar retain the
normalizations in the [signed-channel calculation](C1_SIGNED_SOURCE_CHANNEL_SCREEN.md)
and [absolute angular calculation](C1_ANGULAR_ABSOLUTE_SOURCE_AND_PAIR_BUDGET.md).
The common conversion is \(\eta=2.4127904527582454\,10^{-5}\).

For a radial compartment, with \(a=\log A\) and proper radial derivatives,

\[
\rho_Q-p_{r,Q}
=\frac{\eta c}{48\pi^2R^2}\left(a''+(a')^2\right).
\]

The optical cavity length controls the traceless Casimir part. Population
changes control the magnitude of this additional stress difference.
At \(x=0.75\), the demanded \(\rho-p_r\) after Maxwell is \(+0.00192989\).
Subtracting the retained radial population changes it to \(-0.03031620\).
The whole-module scalar at reference logarithm 1 has positive
\(\rho-p_r\) there. Redistributing radial populations changes that joint
constraint while preserving both source laws.

The angular library uses the absolute one-compartment tensor plus finite
Dirichlet-boundary differences for the other states. Each comparison uses

\[
T^\phi_s(\ell_0)=T^\phi_s(1)-2(\ell_0-1)H,\qquad \ell_0\in\{1,2,4\},
\]

with one fixed physical curvature-coupling case throughout. The
absolute-source report gives its effective-action interpretation and the
compensation required under a change of subtraction convention.
Independent tuning of that coupling at different locations is excluded.

The material allowance obeys the four diagonal dominant-energy
inequalities, \(\rho_M\pm p_{r,M}\geq0\) and
\(\rho_M\pm p_{t,M}\geq0\). These supply a necessary bulk screen.
Material laws, confinement and dynamics remain to be supplied. An assigned
finite cavity interior similarly leaves its external quantum environment
and physical interface to be specified. Arbitrarily multiplying a shared
vacuum tensor by a spatial weight would define a different field problem.

## Population controls and complexity

The four principal probes are
\(x=(-2.4282136055,0,0.75,2.5026707923)\). The first and last lie in the
previously identified transition compartments. Seventy-two comparisons
combine three couplings, four radial population models, three angular
libraries and nominal or empirical-error treatment. The angular choices
are complete module populations, a library also admitting independent
cavities, and a library restricted to independently populated four-,
eight- or 32-compartment cavities.

One uniform central charge configures each group. Right-module cells 8–23
retain their old profile. Equal optical lengths and equal central charge
cancel the radial pressure difference across internal walls within a group.
Changes between groups create explicit traction demands. The independent
compartment model preserves old populations in cells unseen by the probes.

The optimizer first minimizes configured angular populations. Within that
count it minimizes a proxy formed from scalar copy counts and radial
central charges. This compares allocations within a model; source energy,
carrier mass and interface cost require separate budgets. The grouped
model has five adjustable radial populations plus its retained background.
Its microscopic inventory can greatly exceed that of independent cells.

## Four-probe results and boundary demands

At logarithm 1, independent compartment allocation and the five-group radial
construction admit combined allocations. Retained radial populations and
two complete-module scale factors fail the sampled library. At logarithm 2
all four radial controls admit allocations. Logarithm 4 fails after the
transition probes are included.

The library restricted to shorter independent angular cavities fails these
four-probe comparisons. The larger library uses a whole right-module state;
some inventory-proxy optima combine it with a four-compartment left state.
This supports different source states in neighboring modules while exposing
the stress cost of these particular reflective localization choices.

Radial wall tractions are recomputed from both sides of every wall,
\(F_{\rm wall}=-4\pi R^2[p_r]\). The retained maximum scalar radial traction

to 26.4514; its largest change at an individual wall is 27.8198.
These tractions specify local reaction duties. Module recoil depends on
their spatial embedding and complete interactions.

For the logarithm-2 retained-radial example, the left four-compartment
angular state adds \(+3.9085\) at the original left end and \(-0.0041833\)
at the original right end relative to the same population in the long
state. These are finite original-end increments. Absolute scalar-wall
material stress, new internal-wall loads and exterior matching remain
unclosed. Scalar-copy count alone consequently cannot select the
lowest-cost physical assembly.

## Nearby angular states and six-probe allocation

Independent absolute calculations at \(x=0.01\) and \(x=0.05\) test nearby
points in the sampled throat compartment and its neighbor. Twelve regulator
calculations use masses 2, 4 and 8 with cutoff multipliers 16 and 32.
Using both cutoffs at every mass resolves an earlier cutoff-induced shift
in the small radial-null-stress combination. The final trace discrepancies
are \(2.42\,10^{-11}\) and \(4.56\,10^{-11}\), and regulator-fit spreads
remain below \(1.62\,10^{-12}\) per component.

Frozen four-probe, whole-module angular witnesses with independently
adjusted or grouped radial populations fail a material inequality at the
nearby points. The largest observed failure among those frozen witnesses
is about \(5.8\,10^{-6}\). Consequently their populations are reallocated
against all six points. This comparison keeps both angular states
transparent through the radial walls.

These classifications agree for the nominal tensors and the specified

At logarithm 1, a six-probe grouped example uses approximately 365,370 left

to 30.7594 and the largest change at one wall to 32.1278. The logarithm-2

## Spatial coverage and next construction

Dense arrays on 2,049- and 8,193-point base grids record the tensor still
required after Maxwell and each supplied radial allocation, including
both limits near radial walls. They contain residual source duties; the
angular RSET is calculated at the declared probes.

The logarithm-1 grouped allocation leaves a large \(\rho-p_r\) duty near
the upper boundary of its central radial population, \(x\simeq0.51785\).
The four-probe allocation reaches about \(-0.333\) there, and the
six-probe allocation about \(-0.387\), before adding the angular field.
This measured location motivates a more selective
radial extent and an angular-state calculation there. Population-group
boundaries and actual source overlap remain available design variables.

The next construction should resolve the normalized angular contribution
across that population boundary and the nearby overlap, together with
tractions from any changed population or cavity boundary. This follows
the [agreed workflow](SOURCE_FEASIBILITY_WORKFLOW.md): compare spatial
allocations of the existing laws before adding a mechanism solely to repair
a spatial mismatch. The finite pair also requires material tensors,
exterior states, exchanged momentum and module motion.

## Numerical evidence and reproducibility

Twenty-four absolute transition calculations extend the regulator sequence
through masses 16, 24 and 32. Cutoff pairs, radial shooting, interpolation
and frequency controls are retained. The final unconverted one-field
transition tensors at logarithm 1 are

\[
T_L=(-0.0018850082,\;0.0005655158,\;-0.0006997117),\qquad
T_R=(-0.0041725123,\;0.0003264366,\;-0.0015120817).
\]

Absolute trace discrepancies are \(2.63\,10^{-5}\) and \(1.87\,10^{-5}\).
The largest linear/quadratic regulator-fit differences are
\(2.99\,10^{-6}\) and \(2.17\,10^{-6}\). Component sensitivity envelopes
include these discrepancies and observed numerical controls; they quantify
empirical sensitivity while physical finite-margin construction remains open.

Eighteen finite-response calculations cover the four-compartment angular
state. The fine-grid Ward residual reaches 1.20% of the radius-scaled
response at the right transition; the corresponding coarse value is 11.96%.
The largest final-quarter harmonic-tail fraction is \(1.90\,10^{-7}\).
Field differences are independently traceless in the retained prescription.

The 58 C1 tests pass, including independent source reconstruction,
finite-state normalization, source conservation, physical wall exchange and
uniform-group internal-traction cancellation. The audit checks 72
four-probe cases, 24 six-probe cases and 28 dense residual artifacts.
