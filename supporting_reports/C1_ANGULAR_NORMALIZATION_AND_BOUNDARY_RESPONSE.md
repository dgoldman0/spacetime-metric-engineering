# C1 angular source: normalization, reflection and the retained source budget

17 September 2026.

The separate conformal scalar has a physically normalized, helpful change in
angular stress at the throat when internal reflection is introduced. The same
reflection produces substantially larger stresses and material reaction duties
elsewhere. Consequently the next construction prioritizes angular-field
transparency through short radial compartments, together with a specified
whole-module or shared angular state and its finite end response.

The net benefit of the complete angular field remains open. This calculation
supplies a finite vacuum-stress **difference** on the retained geometry and
the corresponding change in original-end traction. Its absolute reference
vacuum, new reflector self/material stresses and physical exterior remain
explicit terms in the source equation. This advances the
[agreed source-feasibility investigation](SOURCE_FEASIBILITY_WORKFLOW.md) from
spectra to normalized stress and a measured boundary burden.

The subsequent [absolute whole-module calculation](C1_ANGULAR_ABSOLUTE_SOURCE_AND_PAIR_BUDGET.md)
supplies bulk reference tensors at the throat and a broad-overlap witness.
It preserves angular transparency through the internal radial walls and
identifies fixed global curvature-coupling cases that help both sampled
source budgets. Its finite end/material/exterior construction remains open.

## What is calculated

The action is the previously specified real, massless four-dimensional scalar
with curvature coupling \(1/6\). Each independent real field carries the
inherited conversion \(\eta=2.4127904527582454\times10^{-5}\). Define

\[
\Delta T_n=\langle T_\phi\rangle_n-\langle T_\phi\rangle_1,
\qquad n\in\{8,32\}.
\]

Both terms use the same geometry, field action, original module endpoints
and stationary vacuum prescription. The new internal Dirichlet conditions
change the state. Away from new walls, local bulk subtraction terms cancel,
including the common curvature counterterms and the conformal anomaly.
Thus this difference has a fixed one-field normalization independently of
the unresolved absolute renormalization constants. At original endpoints,
the common boundary singularities cancel as well; the changed normal traction
has a finite limit.

In the original scalar variable, the Euclidean radial Green operator is

\[
L_{\zeta j}=-\partial_x\left(\frac{AR^2}{B}\partial_x\right)
+AB\left[j(j+1)+R^2\left(\frac{\zeta^2}{A^2}
+\frac{\mathcal R}{6}\right)\right].
\]

For the reference Green function \(g\), added Dirichlet walls \(w_i\) give

\[
\Delta g(x,x')=-g(x,w_i)[g(w_i,w_j)]^{-1}g(w_j,x').
\]

The nearest added walls isolate the compartment containing a probe; more
distant walls produce the same interior solution. The mode measure is

\[
\frac{\eta}{4\pi^2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(2j+1)
\int_0^\infty d\zeta.
\]

The stress includes the conformal improvement

\[
\Delta T_{\mu\nu}=\Delta T^{\rm minimal}_{\mu\nu}
+\frac16\left[G_{\mu\nu}f+g_{\mu\nu}\Box f
-\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu f\right],\qquad f=\Delta\langle\phi^2\rangle.
\]

For example, the Euclidean continuation contributes
\(-\zeta^2\Delta g/A^2\) to the time-derivative moment. The coincident
second radial derivative follows from the homogeneous field equation. The
calculation retains all three orthonormal stresses and checks the bulk trace
and conservation of the difference.

This boundary-response construction follows the scattering Green-function
approach of [Milton and Wagner](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0712.3811).
The complete field-and-material accounting also retains renormalized isolated
surface energies, as developed in
[Milton, Shajesh, Fulling and Parashar](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.0784).
An integral through a newly introduced ideal wall requires those additional
terms; off-wall samples alone supply no total assembly energy.

## Normalization against the retained radial source

The native throat remainder after the fixed Maxwell and radial sources is,
for the broad pair,

\[
(\rho,p_r,p_t)_{\rm rem}\simeq
(0.000477588,-0.000477499,-0.009054029).
\]

Its angular-null projection is approximately \(-0.00857644\). The
separate angular source therefore has a substantial duty after the retained
radial candidate is included.

For a scale comparison, the exact constant-cylinder tensor from
[Butcher, equation (59)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.1283) is

\[
T_{\rm cyl}=\frac{\eta N}{2880\pi^2R^4}
(-2\ell,2\ell,1-2\ell),\qquad \ell=\log(R/a_0).
\]

At the native throat radius \(R_0=2.0395740133\), one real field with
\(\ell=1\) supplies angular-null stress \(-1.47160\times10^{-10}\).
Matching the throat's remaining angular duty with this cylinder tensor and
an ordinary aggregate obeying the dominant energy condition gives the
following local comparison:

| Fixed logarithm at the throat | Broad-pair comparison: minimum integer fields in the throat module |
|---|---:|
| 0.5 | 174,838,720 |
| 1 | 58,279,574 |
| 2 | 24,976,960 |
| 4 | 11,655,915 |

The narrow-pair value at logarithm 1 is 58,279,110. These are benchmark
multiplicities, with the full cylinder tensor and its anomaly retained.
The actual curved-state multiplicity requires the absolute native tensor.
The throat's cylindrical indicator is 0.01185; it supplies an applicability
diagnostic while the finite state remains nonlocal. The parameter \(a_0\)
belongs to one fixed renormalization prescription and physical curvature
couplings. It cannot be fitted independently at each position.

## Useful local response and its spatial cost

On the actual retained geometry, the added angular-null stress per real
field at the throat is:

| Throat module | One to eight compartments | One to thirty-two compartments |
|---|---:|---:|
| Broad left | (-3.447\times10^{-11}) | (-3.467\times10^{-11}) |
| Narrow left | (-1.408\times10^{-11}) | (-3.603\times10^{-11}) |

The negative sign assists the existing angular duty. For the broad module,
changing from 8 to 32 compartments increases this useful throat response by
only about 0.6%. The 8-compartment pattern already includes the nearby
reflector responsible for most of that local response.

The rest of the tensor and its placement determine the combined cost. At
\(x=0.75\), both module populations are present. With one field per module,
their summed 32-compartment response is approximately

\[
\Delta T_{32}^{\rm broad}=(-4.00\times10^{-7},
-1.31\times10^{-6},4.53\times10^{-7}),
\]

\[
\Delta T_{32}^{\rm narrow}=(-4.23\times10^{-7},
-1.37\times10^{-6},4.75\times10^{-7}).
\]

Both have positive angular-null increments, about \(5.3\times10^{-8}\)
per field in each module. Their positive \(\rho-p_r\) increments also increase
the remaining ordinary-material/angular-source burden in that channel.
Consequently the throat's useful sign provides a local contribution; its
extension across the overlap must retain the complete tensor.

## Finite boundary and material ledger

At an original Dirichlet endpoint, \(f=f'=0\). Its finite response has

\[
(\Delta\rho,\Delta p_r,\Delta p_t)
=\Delta b\,(1/6,1/2,-1/6),
\]

where \(\Delta b\) is the mixed radial-derivative moment. The signed
area-integrated traction increment is \(-4\pi R^2\Delta p_r\) at the left
end and \(+4\pi R^2\Delta p_r\) at the right end. This is the same scalar
radial-force convention used by the retained radial source. Center-of-mass
recoil requires the assembly's spatial force and exchange law.

The throat module alone already acquires a substantial load. Applying its
logarithm-1 benchmark multiplicity to the exactly normalized response gives:

| Throat module, left endpoint | Retained radial force | Added angular force, 8 compartments | Added angular force, 32 compartments |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Broad left | 9.0300 | about 2,741 | about 1,510,000 |
| Narrow left | 7.4537 | about 15,219 | about 5,766,000 |

These increments occur in the module containing the throat, so lowering the
neighbor's field population leaves them in its own boundary ledger. For the
shared right module, the additional right-end force per real field is about
\(-0.004318\) with 8 compartments and \(-1.273\) with 32. If that module
also carries the same approximately 58-million-field population, these
increments become about \(-252,000\) and \(-74.2\) million, respectively;
its retained radial force is \(-5.8321\). The common population supplies an
illustrative pair comparison; the throat test constrains its own module.

The full source and endpoint ledgers are therefore

\[
T_{\rm required}=T_{\rm Maxwell}+T_{\rm radial}
+\sum_i N_i\big(T_{\phi,1,i}+\Delta T_{n,i}\big)
+T_{\rm material}+T_{\rm transition/exterior},
\]

\[
F_{\rm end}=F_{\rm retained\ radial}
+N_i\Delta F_{n,i}+N_iF_{\phi,1,i}
+F_{\rm other\ counted\ sectors}.
\]

The computed entries are the retained Maxwell/radial tensors, normalized
off-wall differences, pair overlap sums and original-end force increments.
The open entries are the absolute angular reference state, new reflector
self/material stress, the complete holding and reaction paths, and the
physical exterior/transition tensor. Thus the large increments establish a
measured confinement burden while the complete assembly comparison remains
dependent on those entries.

A separate exact product-cylinder control counts the finite interaction of
two Dirichlet ends. Its angular modes have
\(m_j=\sqrt{j(j+1)+1/3}/R\), and its interaction energy is

\[
E_{\rm int}=-\frac{\eta}{2\pi}
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2j+1)m_j}{n}
K_1(2nm_jd).
\]

For a direct static axial support in this constant cylinder, ordinary matter
obeying the dominant energy condition has holding energy at least
\(d|F_{\rm int}|\). Each positive-mass summand satisfies

\[
\frac{d|F|}{|E|}=1+z\frac{K_0(z)}{K_1(z)}>1,
\qquad z=2nm_jd.
\]

Hence \(E_{\rm int}+d|F_{\rm int}|>0\) for this held interaction control,
even with zero additional end rest energy. Seven length/radius comparisons
from 0.1 to 8 verify the sum and its virtual-work force. This bound concerns
the end interaction and a specified direct support; the curvature vacuum and
other C1 support or exchange arrangements retain their own tensors and costs.

## Construction decision

Continue the angular scalar as a separately assigned source candidate with
field-selective coupling. Prioritize its whole-module or shared through-going
state, one physical renormalization prescription, and finite end/exterior
response. The 32-compartment radial population remains available in its own
role. Further blanket angular reflection at the same short compartments
requires a demonstrated combined benefit against the measured added loads.

The next decisive calculation is the absolute angular reference contribution
with a specified material response and exterior, combined with the retained
radial and ordinary sectors. Detailed fixture work follows that combined
source balance. If this calculation motivates changes to the geometry,
field extent, reflector location or overlap, those changes receive their own
declared comparison and service checks.

## Reproduction and numerical evidence

The registered response comparisons, harmonic contributions, retained-source
reconstruction and force ledger are in the
[data bundle](data/c1_angular_response/summary.json). The
[specification](../toolkit/adm_harness_cli/specs/c1_angular_response.json)

The production sweep contains 66 comparisons and supports independent work
with four processes and one BLAS thread per process.

The validation uses analytic cylinder Green functions, conformal covariance,
clock normalization, a Bessel-sum energy/force comparison, the bulk Ward
identity and an independent consistent-mass solve in \(u=R\phi\). The
[audit](data/c1_angular_response/audit.json) records the numerical errors and
checks production hashes. Forty focused tests cover this response, the scalar
spectrum and the retained C1 radial/Maxwell components.

The 84 independent operator comparisons agree within 0.109% in the largest
tensor component norm. Production mesh/frequency refinement changes the
sampled tensor by at most 0.164%; separate frequency and metric controls give
relative changes below \(2.3\times10^{-12}\) and \(4.4\times10^{-7}\).
The last quarter of the retained angular sum contributes below
\(2.5\times10^{-7}\) of the tensor norm.

Conservation is checked separately across resolved bulk intervals. The
closely spaced production probes amplify derivative truncation error, with
a maximum radius-scaled residual of 0.465. Thirty-two wider-stencil spatial
controls cover all 16 interior witnesses. Their finest residual is at most
0.0105, while their central tensor differs from production by at most 0.047%.
The archived evidence therefore supports sampled stress and force comparisons
at the quoted precision; the continuous stress profile retains a finite
derivative error.
